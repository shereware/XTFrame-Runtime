# NPDO: National Property Disposal Officer — story bible (English)

Translated from the author's Traditional Chinese copy (`npdo-story-zh.md`, the source of truth). Translator's notes are in *italics*.

## 1. Premise

You are an officer of the National Property Administration (國有財產署). Whenever the government sells, develops or otherwise puts land to use, you are the one who disposes of it.

Today's assignment: survey a structure standing on land once leased by a now-dissolved legal entity — a hospital, built and opened in **1991** and abandoned in **1997** — and decide how to dispose of it, so the land can be revitalised under government policy.

What follows is a chain of terrifying events.

## 2. Setting — surface lore (表設定)

- The hospital was built with investment from a **foundation** (財團法人), which brought in high-end, cutting-edge equipment and staff.
- It is not large. It consists of two connected buildings:
  - the **Surgical Building** (外科大樓), renowned for cardiac surgery;
  - the **Internal Medicine & Psychosomatic Medicine Building** (內科暨心身醫學科大樓), focused on frontier treatments.
- **Twenty years ago** an inexplicable hostage incident took place. For some reason there were few hostages. A police assault failed for no clear reason, and the incident ended with several members of the Thunder Squad (霹靂小組, the police special-operations unit) missing. The site has been sealed off ever since.

## 3. Setting — hidden lore (裏設定)

- Beneath the hospital ran research into **bio-energy** (生體能量): a phenomenon discovered by a certain group and turned into a religion. The stated aim was to make the theory scientific and bring it into public view.
- It was led and funded by an entrepreneur who had already turned their own company into a religion.
- Despite that, the real goal was never to separate bio-energy from religion — it was **control**. What grew out of it was goal-driven experimentation: a clear result was the reference, and the process was disregarded. The experiments included:
  - drawing energy out of people;
  - infusing energy into healthy bodies;
  - infusing energy into the sick;
  - infusing energy into the dead;
  - drawing energy out of animals;
  - using energy during surgery.
- Although they had technology able to harness bio-energy to a degree, they deliberately ignored its essence: **bio-energy arises for the sake of survival, and exists because of survival.**

## 4. Story

The player comes to survey the hospital and ends up imprisoned inside it. What waits there:

- obsessions left behind by experiments that showed no reverence for life;
- leftover monsters released from hidden rooms, twisted in mind and body;
- people who broke in to explore and were assimilated and fused;
- souls that people call ghosts — they have nothing to do with the bio-energy research and were always there.

The player must escape this hostile place alive.

## 5. Gameplay

### Route 1 — survive

Among the hospital's original mechanisms, beings that still keep the ability to think, and an environment rotted by time, the player must hold their breath, hide their fear and use their wits: evade the wandering monsters, disarm the mechanisms, learn what happened here — and leave alive, then try to bring people back to destroy this place.

### Route 2 — fight back

Those rotten, twisted things, overflowing with life energy that isn't theirs, are terrifying. What? You say life energy comes from the activity and the will of the living? Then rise up! Let your rage burn! In this place where you are the only one truly alive, you must never fear the ghosts — **the ghosts should fear you!**

*Notes on the items:*

- *"Mouth cannon" (嘴砲) is Taiwanese slang for trash-talk / empty boasting; the joke is that here it is literal. Keep the pun, or localise it (e.g. "talk about a big mouth!").*
- *"Disposal" (處分) is the officer's administrative term for getting rid of state land. The MP5 line turns it into a pun on "dealing with" the monsters — the title NPDO carries the same double meaning.*
